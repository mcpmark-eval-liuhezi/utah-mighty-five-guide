## What it is

Massive cream-, pink-, and red-colored sandstone cliffs, the tall narrow walls of slot canyons, and the Virgin River at the bottom of it all. Zion Canyon — where most campsites and popular hikes are — is reached through the South Entrance near Springdale, and for most of the year the canyon's scenic drive is **shuttle-bus only**. Day/night temperatures can differ by more than 30 °F.

## Campgrounds & amenities

### Watchman Campground — **the one that's open in November**

- **Sites:** 184 total (176 reservable; 69 tent-only, 18 tent-only walk-in, 92 with electrical hookups, 6 group)
- **Fee:** $35/night non-electric · $45 electric · group sites $50–$130
- **Amenities:** flush toilets (year-round), **potable water (year-round)**, picnic tables, fire rings, trash/recycling, food storage, dump station, amphitheater, camp host, cell reception, dump station; **no showers**
- **Location:** next to the Zion Canyon Visitor Center, ¼ mile from the South Entrance — walkable to shuttle Stop 1
- **Reservations:** **required for every site; there are no first-come, first-served sites.** Book up to **6 months ahead** on recreation.gov. Open year-round (some loops may close seasonally for weather/maintenance).
- **Regulations:** check-out 11 am, quiet hours 10 pm–8 am, **generators not permitted at all**, no hammocks on trees.

### South Campground

- Between the visitor center and Nature Center, ½ mile from the South Entrance. 126-ish sites; picnic tables, fire rings, food storage boxes. Reservations only, up to 2 weeks ahead.
- **⚠️ Campsites are typically available mid-March through October 31 — CLOSED for our dates.**

### Lava Point Campground

- 6 primitive tent-only sites at **7,890 ft**, reached via Kolob Terrace Road (25 mi / ~45 min north of Virgin; ~80 min from the South Entrance). Pit toilets and trash cans, **no running water**. Reservations required up to 14 days ahead; no FCFS. Vehicles over 19 ft not permitted.
- **⚠️ Typically open May through September, as weather allows — CLOSED for our dates.**

**Our pick: Watchman Campground.** It's the only Zion campground reliably open in early November, it has year-round potable water and flush toilets, and its location next to the visitor center puts us at shuttle Stop 1 — meaning we don't have to fight for parking or move the car once we're settled. Reservations are mandatory, so this is the one campground on the whole trip we must lock in early.

## Events: November 1–14, 2026

**One recurring program runs every single day of our window:**

### 🎤 Zion Canyon Visitor Center Program (daily ranger talk)

- **Dates:** runs daily, including **every day from Nov 1 through Nov 14, 2026** (the listing runs Oct 19 – Nov 28)
- **What:** a short ranger talk on the visitor center plaza, right next to the shuttle stop — easy to catch before or after riding the Zion Canyon Shuttle. "Topics may include geology, wildlife, plants, human history, and so much more. No two programs are the same!"
- **Where:** outdoors at the Zion Canyon Visitor Center amphitheater (across the plaza from the front doors). Partially shaded patio, stone benches, space for mobility devices.

Beyond that, no special events are calendared for Nov 1–14. This is a nice, low-effort daily ritual rather than something to build a day around — for us, the headline "events" at Zion are the canyon itself and the shuttle timing.

## Notes from the park website (checked late October 2026)

1. **The shuttle is running during our dates, and private cars are banned from Zion Canyon Scenic Drive.** Per the [shuttle page](https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/zion-canyon-shuttle-system.htm), for **October 25 – November 28, 2026**: first shuttle leaves the visitor center **7:00 am**; last shuttle leaves the visitor center **5:00 pm**; last shuttle out of the canyon from Temple of Sinawava **6:15 pm**. The page is emphatic that **you do not need a permit, ticket, or reservation to enter the park or ride the shuttle** — just pay the fee or show a pass. But during shuttle season "visitors **cannot drive personal vehicles** on the Zion Canyon Scenic Drive… including before shuttles start for the day *and* after they stop running." Buses run every 5–10 minutes on the canyon line; a one-way ride to Temple of Sinawava takes ~45 minutes. **"You should not wait for the last shuttle of the day"** — miss it and you may walk nine miles. (Shuttle service then pauses Nov 29 – Dec 25.) You *can* still drive the Zion–Mt. Carmel Highway/SR-9, Kolob Terrace Road, and Kolob Canyons Scenic Drive.

2. **Active alerts (5 posted, as of Oct 19, 2026):**
   - **Watchman Trail is closed** pending repairs (closure, posted Aug 31, 2026) — a bummer since it's the classic campground-adjacent sunset hike; Pa'rus Trail and the Canyon Junction bridge remain great alternatives.
   - **Toxic cyanobacteria in rivers, streams, and pools** — do not submerge your head in or drink instream water. The Narrows closes when the Virgin River exceeds 150 cfs or when a Flash Flood Warning is issued.
   - **Large vehicle restrictions on the Zion–Mt. Carmel Highway** — vehicles over length/width/height/weight limits cannot travel between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance.
   - **Parking:** Zion has limited parking; once full, park in Springdale and ride the free Springdale Line shuttle. Illegally parked vehicles are cited and towed.
   - **Angels Landing Pilot Permit Program — all hikers need a permit** to hike Angels Landing.
   *(Source: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)*

3. **Long-term trail closures:** the East Rim Trail is partially closed due to a large rockfall — **Hidden Canyon is closed**, and **Observation Point cannot be reached from Zion Canyon** (only by hiking in from the east). Plan the classic hikes accordingly. *(Source: same conditions page.)*
