## What it is

A hidden-treasure park in south-central Utah filled with cliffs, canyons, domes, and natural bridges along the **Waterpocket Fold** — a nearly 100-mile geologic monocline, a wrinkle in the earth's crust. It's the least-visited of the Mighty Five, with pioneer-era orchards in the historic Fruita district. Weather note from the park: rain and snow can make dirt roads impassable, and you shouldn't enter narrow canyons when there's a threat of rain.

## Campgrounds & amenities

### Fruita Campground — the only developed campground in the park

- **Sites:** 71 (65 reservable, incl. 7 tent-only/walk-in); located in the Fruita historic district beside the Fremont River
- **Fee:** $25/night
- **Amenities:** **flush toilets (year-round)**, **potable water (year-round)**, picnic tables, fire rings, restrooms, dump station, trash/recycling, food storage lockers, camp host, cell reception, paved loops and paved restroom paths
- **Reservations:** **open year-round and reservable year-round** via recreation.gov, up to **6 months in advance** — no FCFS sites. Winter demand is low, so sites are easy to get in November.
- **Regulations:** generators permitted only in the C loop (in A and B when C is closed for winter); backcountry camping requires a free permit from the visitor center.

### Group Campsite (near Fruita)

- 1 site, max 40 people, $125/night, reservable up to 1 year ahead via recreation.gov. **Open mid-April to mid-October only — closed for our dates.** Closed every Tuesday and Wednesday night for maintenance.

### Primitive campgrounds (no fee, no reservations, first-come, first-served)

| Campground | Sites | Amenities | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cathedral Valley (Cathedral Campground)** | 6 | picnic table + fire grate per site, pit (vault) toilet, **no water**; ~7,000 ft elevation, ~36 miles from the visitor center on the Cathedral Valley Loop Road | **Unpaved — 4WD/high clearance required**; check road conditions with the visitor center first |
| **Cedar Mesa Campground** | 5 | picnic table + fire grate per site, pit toilet, **no water**; 5,500 ft, ~23 miles south of UT-24 on the Notom-Bullfrog Road | **Unpaved — 4WD/high clearance required**; check road conditions first |

**Our pick: Fruita Campground** — no contest. Year-round water and flush toilets, paved loops, reservable (and uncrowded in November), and you're camping among century-old orchards with the Fremont River trail out the door. The primitive sites are genuinely remote and need high clearance plus a road-conditions check.

## Notes from the park website (checked late October 2026)

1. **The park is fully open**, 24 hours a day. Visitor center hours: spring-through-fall **8:00 am – 4:30 pm** daily; **winter hours (beginning Nov 29) 9:00 am – 4:30 pm** — our trip lands before the change, so 8:00 am openings. The **Capitol Gorge road is currently CLOSED** (per the Oct 18, 2026 road-conditions table), and **Notom-Bullfrog is HC4WD** (high-clearance four-wheel-drive). Scenic Drive and Grand Wash were listed as 2WD/passable. Road conditions on the website are updated weekly — the park says its **recorded phone line (435-425-3791) has the most current information.** *(Source: https://www.nps.gov/care/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)*

2. **Water-quality caution:** recent testing shows **elevated E. coli in the Fremont River and Sulphur Creek** (warning advisory from Utah DEQ, posted Aug 28, 2026): use caution, don't get water in your mouth, don't swim, wash hands with soap and water. There's also a standing **monsoon/flash-flood caution** — heavy rain can cause sudden flooding in canyons and washes; don't drive or hike through flood water. *(Source: same conditions page.)*

3. **Fruita Campground is reservable year-round** through recreation.gov (6 months ahead; 1 year for the group site) — one of the few campgrounds on this trip where November booking is genuinely easy. *(Source: same conditions page + campground record.)*
