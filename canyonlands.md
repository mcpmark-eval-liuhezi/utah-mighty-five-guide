## What it is

A wilderness of canyons, buttes, and spires carved by the Colorado and Green Rivers. The park is split into **four distinct districts that no roads connect** — Island in the Sky, The Needles, The Maze, and the Rivers (plus the separate Horseshoe Canyon unit) — so visiting "Canyonlands" means choosing districts and driving between them. Island in the Sky is the classic first visit; The Maze requires high-clearance 4WD and a lot of time.

## Campgrounds & amenities

### The Needles Campground

- **Sites:** 26 individual (5 tent-only) + 3 group sites; at the end of UT 211 in The Needles district
- **Fee:** $20/night standard site (max 10 people, 2 vehicles)
- **Amenities:** flush toilets (seasonal) + vault toilets (year-round), potable water (seasonal), picnic tables, fire rings, trash/recycling, amphitheater, dump station, food storage lockers, camp host, cell reception
- **Reservations:** Loop B reservable up to 6 months ahead **for nights March 15 – November 15**; **Loop A is first-come, first-served only**; group sites open Mar 15–Nov 15. 14 of 26 sites are FCFS.
- **Winter note:** Loop A is open year-round; Loop B closes when the park quiets down for winter.
- **RVs:** up to 28 ft; paved access roads.

### Island in the Sky (Willow Flat) Campground

- **Sites:** 12, **all first-come, first-served** (no reservations taken); ~22 miles west on UT 313, near the Green River Overlook
- **Fee:** $15/night
- **Amenities:** toilets, picnic tables, fire rings, trash/recycling — **⚠️ there is NO water at the campground** (fill up outside the visitor center spring through fall)
- Open year-round; sites fill most days spring through fall, but November demand is low.

**Our pick: The Needles Campground.** It has water, flush toilets in season, 26 sites with 14 first-come, and it's still inside the reservation window (Loop B bookable through **Nov 15** — we'd be among the last nights of the season). The trade-off is that it's ~90 minutes from Moab and separate from Island in the Sky; if we base ourselves on Island in the Sky instead, Willow Flat works but we must carry all our own water and there are only 12 FCFS sites. A good compromise: one night at Willow Flat for the Green River Overlook sunset, one at The Needles.

## Events: November 1–14, 2026

**One event — and it's a genuinely great one to build a day around:**

### 🥾 Horseshoe Canyon Guided Walk

- **Dates:** Saturdays — Oct 24, Oct 31, and **Saturday, November 7, 2026**
- **Time:** meet the ranger at **9:00 am** at the Horseshoe Canyon trailhead
- **What:** a four-hour guided hike into Horseshoe Canyon with stops at four major pictograph panels, **including the Great Gallery**, where the ranger-led portion concludes. Visitors return to the trailhead on their own.
- **Difficulty:** strenuous — 780 ft descent (and ascent back), long stretches of loose sand, limited shade. The full hike to the Great Gallery is 7 miles round trip and takes five hours or more.
- **Bring:** one gallon (4 L) of water per person (no water at the trailhead), snacks/lunch, sturdy shoes, sun protection. No pets below the rim.
- **Group size:** limited to 20 people.
- **Info:** call the Hans Flat Ranger Station at 435-241-3105 for current road conditions.

**Why it matters for our route:** the Horseshoe Canyon trailhead is off UT-24 between Green River and Hanksville — essentially **on the drive from Moab to Capitol Reef**. Doing the Nov 7 walk turns our transfer day into the trip's best cultural experience.
